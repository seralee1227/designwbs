# Mobile App Design (iOS/Android) UI/UX Trends — Late November 2026

## Have Apple or Google published/updated any design guidance recently (HIG, Liquid Glass successor styles, Material Design)?

### Takeaway
Apple's Human Interface Guidelines saw only minor, resource-level updates in November 2026 (new device bezels, refreshed Figma/Sketch kits) with no new Liquid Glass guidance; the more substantive HIG content changes (macOS touch/gesture guidance, app icon formats, widget/Live Activity layout rules) landed in late October and are the most recent true guideline updates. On Android, Google is still iterating Material 3 Expressive rather than releasing a new design generation — the most recent concrete change is an Expressive redesign of the time-picker component, shipped via Jetpack Compose's November 2026 release.

### Cited Findings
- Apple's Design "What's New" page lists a **November 6, 2026** update adding new product bezels for the MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display, iPad mini, and Apple Watch Ultra 4, and a refresh of the **Live Activities UI kits for Figma and Sketch**, now updated for iOS 27 and including macOS components/examples for the first time — [Apple Developer: What's New](https://developer.apple.com/design/whats-new/)
- The same page shows no other HIG content changes in November 2026; the most recent substantive HIG updates are from **October 28, 2026** (new guidance on touch input and gestures for macOS, including revealing hidden controls with touch on Mac; updated guidance for app icon format options) and **October 13, 2026** (widgets guidance for Apple HomeView and updated watchOS specs; Live Activities guidance for limited-width layouts) — [Apple Developer: What's New](https://developer.apple.com/design/whats-new/)
- No Liquid Glass-specific HIG changes appear in the November or late-October entries; the most recent Liquid Glass guidance updates found were from mid-2026, tied to the iOS/iPadOS/macOS 27 cycle — [Apple Developer: What's New](https://developer.apple.com/design/whats-new/)
- Design kits for iOS, iPadOS, and macOS 27 (Figma/Sketch) were previously released with updated Liquid Glass components, expanded component/state support, naming changes to align with code, and improved resizing — [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news?id=e2lxw9l1)
- Apple's design leadership changed in **December 2025**: Alan Dye (VP of Human Interface Design) left for Meta; Steve Lemay, a 1999-era Apple designer, was named his successor. Apple's own leadership page lists Lemay as VP of Human Interface Design. This predates the "last 1-2 weeks" window but is relevant context for why HIG updates have been incremental rather than a visual overhaul — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/12/04/john-gruber-on-alan-dye), [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/12/04/dyes-departure-doesnt-mean-liquid-glass-is-going-anywhere), [Apple Leadership](https://www.apple.com/leadership/steve-lemay/)
- On Android: a report dated **November 17, 2026** describes Google updating the time-picker components in Material Design 3 documentation — adding a compact dial variant and higher-contrast elements, with AM/PM toggles that change shape and color on press when pressed; these updated components are now available for Jetpack Compose — [comss.ru (Russian-language report)](https://www.comss.ru/page.php?id=22938); corroborated in general terms (Expressive redesign of the time picker, adding a scrolling-dial variant, dropping the "Select time" hint text) by [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/guides/material-design/) coverage, though the exact publish date of that specific 9to5Google piece could not be confirmed in search results — treat the November 17 date as sourced to the single Russian-language aggregator only.
- Google's Android Developers blog announced the **Jetpack Compose "November '26" release** as stable, bundling **Material 3 version 1.5.0** with new Expressive components, and recommending developers migrate from Material 2 to Material 3 to get current design guidance and fixes — [Android Developers Blog](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/jetpack-compose-november-2026-release)
- Industry commentary (Android Authority) explicitly states Material 3 Expressive is **not** a new generation ("Material 4") but an extension of the existing Material You philosophy — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-material-3-expressive-features-changes-availability-supported-devices-3556392/)
- Google's I/O 2026 technical session on Material Design previewed further Expressive updates to components and greater customizability, rolling out progressively across first-party apps (e.g., Gmail's new search app bar on Android) — [io.google/2026 session listing](https://io.google/2026/explore/technical-session-30); [9to5Google Material Design hub](https://9to5google.com/guides/material-design)

### Inferences
- Both platforms appear to be in a "refinement, not revolution" phase this cycle: Apple is polishing Liquid Glass-era HIG details (icons, widgets, Live Activities, Mac touch input) rather than introducing a new visual language, and Google is extending Material 3 Expressive component-by-component (time picker now, other components previously) rather than shipping a "Material 4."
- The Apple design-kit refresh explicitly adding **macOS** components to a kit historically scoped to Live Activities (an iOS/watchOS concept) suggests Apple is pushing toward more cross-platform consistency in its design resources, consistent with the macOS 27 touch-input guidance added in late October.

## What's trending in mobile UI patterns (widgets, lock/home screen customization, AI assistant interfaces, gesture navigation, onboarding)?

### Takeaway
No single new mobile UI pattern broke out this specific week; the live, dated signal is narrow (Material 3's Expressive time-picker refresh, Apple's design-kit bezel/Live Activities updates), while the broader conversation in design circles remains dominated by two carry-over themes with no fresh news this week: lock-screen/home-screen widget customization (framed as an iOS 27 vs. Android 17 comparison) and AI assistants shifting from reactive chat to anticipatory, cross-app "front door" interfaces.

### Cited Findings
- A comparative piece frames **iOS 27 vs. Android 17** on lock-screen widgets: Android is described as giving users more widget choice and the ability to perform actions without unlocking the phone, with "a surprising amount of variety... in widget design"; the comparison is presented as more evenly matched than earlier iOS 26 vs. Android 16 comparisons, which had favored iOS's customization options — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/phones/android-phones/android-17-vs-ios-27-which-platform-does-lock-screen-widgets-the-best), [Tom's Guide (iOS26 vs Android16)](https://www.tomsguide.com/phones/i-tested-the-new-lock-screen-customization-with-ios-26-vs-android-16-and-theres-a-clear-winner)

- AI-assistant interface commentary (general, not dated to this week) describes a shift of the "front door" of mobile experiences from the app grid/UI to the assistant itself, with Apple and Google positioning Siri and Gemini as the primary way users accomplish tasks; chat interfaces are getting richer (inline charts, action buttons, generated images, code blocks) rather than staying plain text — [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/apple-google-ai-phones-mobile-apps-future), [ArcTouch](https://arctouch.com/blog/ai-app-interface)
