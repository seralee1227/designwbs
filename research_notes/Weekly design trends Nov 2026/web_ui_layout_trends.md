## What's happening with motion design, scroll-based interactions, 3D/WebGL on the web, or AI-generated/AI-assisted interfaces?

### Takeaway
The single most concrete, currently-live story in this space is the ongoing public argument over AI design tools (specifically Anthropic's Claude Design / "Claude" as used for web design) producing a recognizable, homogenous look — nicknamed "Claudian sameness" — which was still being actively discussed by designers and covered by Wix as of **mid-November 2026**, even though the originating New Yorker report and related commentary pieces date back to roughly June–July 2026. Separately, scroll-driven WebGL/3D "experience" sites remain an awards-circuit staple built on GSAP + Three.js + Lenis, with no evidence of a genuinely new technique emerging this specific week.

### Cited Findings
- Wix Studio's VP/GM (Erez) described, in content published around **mid-November 2026**, a running joke in a designers' group about how to stop Claude (Anthropic's AI design tool) from "designing like Claude," with the group's half-joking fix being to explicitly instruct the AI not to do that. — [Wix blog](https://www.wix.com/blog/how-web-designers-use-ai)
- The term "Claudian sameness" originates from a New Yorker piece by Kyle Chayka ("The A.I.-Design Aesthetic That's Taking Over the Internet"), reported via secondary coverage as describing a recognizable house style of beige/cream backgrounds, rusty-orange accents, large italicized serif type, and ticker-style scrolling bars across many AI-generated sites; two founders quoted in the piece confirmed they had used Claude Design, and an Anthropic spokesperson said the tool should ideally be able to move away from a "standard look" when users want that. — [Northeast Times](https://northeasttimes.com/2026/07/31/ai-design-tools-are-making-every-website-look-the-same/); [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.sg/why-so-many-ai-built-websites-are-starting-look-same-91292)
- A UX Collective essay argues AI-generated design's sameness is "a mediocre version" of shared design conventions "applied the same way everywhere," not an emergent aesthetic logic, and cites a March 2026 Anthropic engineering post noting that AI agents tend to over-praise their own mediocre output ("you can't unit-test taste"). — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/ai-design-isnt-ugly-it-s-fluent-and-that-s-the-problem-131b2f4eb78c)
- None of the designers quoted across these pieces reject AI design tools outright; the consensus framing is that a distinctive look remains achievable with AI tools, but requires deliberate human effort/editing rather than accepting default output. — [Northeast Times](https://northeasttimes.com/2026/07/31/ai-design-tools-are-making-every-website-look-the-same/)
- On the technical/craft side, Codrops' July 2026 deep-dive on the "Trionn" site demonstrates the current state-of-the-art combination for award-circuit motion/3D sites: GSAP for animation sequencing, Three.js for 3D/WebGL rendering, Lenis for smooth-scroll, and the Web Audio API for audio-reactive effects, built over roughly four months. — [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/15/the-architecture-behind-trionn-coordinating-gsap-three-js-lenis-and-web-audio/)
- "Agentic UX" / "agentic experience design" is being discussed as a UI pattern category by Salesforce, UX Magazine, and Infosys blogs: moving from interface-centric to intent-centric design, where the user states a goal in plain language, the system previews its intended plan before acting, and autonomy/delegation controls (pause, override, undo) are treated as primary, visible UI elements rather than buried settings — this is framed as an emerging pattern across sources but none of these pieces were dated to the specific Nov 12–26 window. — [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/ux-shift-to-agentic-experience-design/); [UX Magazine](https://uxmag.com/articles/agentic-ux-when-the-interface-stops-waiting-for-you)
- Figma's Config 2026 conference (**June 23–25, 2026**, not within the recent window but relevant background) introduced "code layers" (editable code on the design canvas), native motion/animation tooling directly on canvas, AI-generated shader effects, custom AI "skills," and prompt-built plugins — signaling where AI-assisted interface tooling has been heading into H2 2026. — [Figma blog](https://www.figma.com/blog/config-2026-recap/); [TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/figma-config-code-layers-ai-skills-plugins-animations)

### Inferences
- The "Claudian sameness" debate is less a layout/interaction-technique trend and more a meta-discourse about AI design tools converging on similar visual outputs (beige/cream palettes, oversized italic serif type, ticker bars) — it's the most genuinely "live" and widely-discussed topic touching web UI/UX as of this research window, even though its origin predates the strict two-week cutoff. Its continued citation by Wix in mid-November suggests it has become a durable reference point in design-community conversation rather than a one-off news cycle.
- The lack of any new scroll/3D technique distinct from the GSAP+Three.js+Lenis stack suggests motion/WebGL craft is in a consolidation phase — the toolchain is settled and award-winning sites are differentiating on execution/content rather than on novel technical approaches.
