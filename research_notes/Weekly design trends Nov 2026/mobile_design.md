No major new design *language* was introduced in the last 1–2 weeks by either Apple or Google — Liquid Glass (Apple) and Material 3 Expressive (Google) remain the current systems, both dating to mid-2025/2026 — but Apple's Human Interface Guidelines did receive a dated, narrow update on app icon guidance on **November 6, 2026**, and Google shipped a notable AI-generated-UI feature ("Create My Widget") in its **November 10, 2026** Pixel Feature Drop that is arguably more significant for mobile UI/UX than a guideline change.

## What mobile interaction/navigation patterns are trending (gestures, navigation, dynamic-island-style UI, widgets, AI assistant UI)?

### Takeaway
The dominant, most concretely-sourced trend in the past two weeks is **AI-generated/AI-assembled widgets and agentic in-app automation** (Google's Create My Widget and Gemini's multi-step app actions); beyond that, broader 2026 roundups (mostly vendor/agency blogs, treat as directional rather than authoritative) point to glanceable widget/Live Activity-style status surfaces, compound/layered gestures, and AI assistants embedded as sidebars, floating modals, or inline contextual suggestions rather than full-screen chat.

### Cited Findings
- **AI-generated widgets**: Google's "Create My Widget" (Nov 10, 2026 Pixel Drop) lets a user type a natural-language description and have Gemini generate a home-screen widget pulling live data from Gmail, Calendar, and Search — described by one reviewer as building "a resizable dashboard you can drop on the home screen." — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/10/november-2026-pixel-feature-drop/)
- **Agentic AI acting inside other apps**: Gemini's multi-step automation lets it act through third-party Android apps on the user's behalf (e.g., preparing food orders, ride requests, restaurant bookings) — expanded to the UK, India, and Japan on November 10, 2026; still labeled "beta." — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/googles-gemini-can-now-use-food-and-ride-apps-for-people-in-3-more-countries/)
- **AI assistant UI placement patterns** (from design-practitioner sources, not dated to November specifically): common patterns identified are (1) floating modal/corner-button chat overlays, (2) inline contextual suggestions appearing directly in content (cited examples: Notion AI, Grammarly), (3) sidebar/split-screen copilots, and (4) "ambient"/ intent-based agentic systems and canvas-based AI workspaces as emerging alternatives to chat-only UI. — [UX Design (uxdesign.cc)](https://uxdesign.cc/where-should-ai-sit-in-your-ui-1710a258390e); [Lazarev Agency](https://www.lazarev.agency/articles/ai-ui-paradigms-beyond-chat)
- **Adoption data point**: A July 2026 study of 809 apps found AI-assistant screens present in 8.8% of captured apps in 2025, up from 2.4% in 2023 — described by its authors as still "a differentiator, not yet an expectation," with the figure characterized as a likely lower bound. — [lazyweb.com research](https://www.lazyweb.com/research/how-fast-did-ai-assistant-screens-spread-in-apps.md)
- **Widgets as glanceable status surfaces**: A practitioner piece argues glanceable status (Live Activities, widgets) is displacing "open the app" flows, citing Apple Sports' Live Activities as an example, and claims Apple and Google are both expanding persistent status surfaces across lock screens, watches, and widgets. — [dev.to](https://dev.to/zdeb_fouc_f95089367652fc5/from-agent-checkpoints-to-spatial-widgets-five-uiux-patterns-that-look-poised-to-define-2026-m3i)
- **Spatial widgets**: Some 2026 trend pieces tie widgets to spatial computing, referencing Apple's visionOS 26 preview making widgets "spatial" and anchored in physical space — this is a visionOS (not phone) context, included for completeness since it signals a cross-platform widget-model direction. — [dev.to](https://dev.to/zdeb_fouc_f95089367652fc5/from-agent-checkpoints-to-spatial-widgets-five-uiux-patterns-that-look-poised-to-define-2026-m3i)
- **Gestures — conflicting signals**: One source frames 2026's gesture direction as "compound gestures with feedback layers," citing Telegram's swipe-to-reply as an example of layered gesture + visual feedback. Another source lists "complex gestures" explicitly as a UI pitfall to avoid in 2026, citing discoverability problems. These two framings directly conflict and should be treated as unsettled practitioner opinion, not consensus. — [dev.to (compound gestures)](https://dev.to/teriann_boisvert_5a7ad677/five-uiux-patterns-quietly-defining-2026-and-the-products-already-shipping-them-1mal); [WebProNews (gesture pitfalls)](https://www.webpronews.com/7-ui-pitfalls-mobile-app-developers-should-avoid-in-2026)
- **WhatsApp's floating nav bar** (see redesign section above) is itself a navigation-pattern trend data point: a detached, rounded, blurred bottom bar — part of a broader move (also seen in iOS tab bars) away from edge-to-edge flat bottom nav toward "floating pill" nav bars. — [WABetaInfo via propakistani.pk](https://propakistani.pk/2026/09/28/whatsapp-to-make-major-interface-change-on-android/)
- **Recurring secondary themes** across multiple vendor/agency 2026 roundups (lower authority, should be read as directional): thumb-friendly layouts and bottom sheets, continued dark-mode investment, micro-interactions, biometric-first login flows, and multimodal input (voice, gesture, device-motion, context-aware). — [Mindinventory](https://www.mindinventory.com/blog/?p=13130); [Design Studio UI/UX](https://www.designstudiouiux.com/blog/mobile-app-ui-ux-design-trends/)

### Inferences
- The clearest throughline from the last two weeks specifically (not just "2026 generally") is that both Apple and Google are pushing AI deeper into system-level mobile surfaces (widgets, cross-app automation) rather than confining AI to an in-app chat window — for a design team, this means widget and home-screen design systems increasingly need to account for AI-assembled/variable content rather than fixed, designer-authored widget layouts.
- The floating/detached bottom-nav-bar pattern (WhatsApp, and broadly iOS's existing tab bar / "Liquid Glass" floating toolbar treatment) appears to be converging across platforms as a shared aesthetic, suggesting cross-platform visual convergence on "floating pill" chrome is a trend worth tracking even though it's not brand-new this month.

### Gaps
- Could not find primary-source (Apple/Google official) documentation of specific new gesture patterns introduced in the last two weeks; all gesture-trend claims come from third-party/vendor blogs with no official platform backing found in this research pass.
- Could not confirm whether "dynamic island"-style UI treatments have seen any recent (November 2026) updates or expansion to new apps/platforms — no sources specifically addressing Dynamic Island changes surfaced in this research.
- Most "trend roundup" sources are vendor/agency content marketing (SEO-oriented blogs) rather than independent journalism or primary platform documentation; conclusions drawn from them should be treated as directional signal, not confirmed fact.
